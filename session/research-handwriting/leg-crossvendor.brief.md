# Research consult — does "handwriting" have an analogue for AI agents?

You are an independent research consultant. This is a blind cross-check: you
receive only this brief, no other context. Answer from your own knowledge.
Be honest about uncertainty — "unknown" is a valid and valued answer.

## Background (all the context you get)

For humans, writing by hand appears to aid thinking through several candidate
mechanisms:

1. **Forced compression** — the hand is slower than thought; you must select
   and condense rather than transcribe.
2. **Motor–semantic coupling** — handwriting engages motor and perceptual
   brain systems beyond language areas.
3. **Ephemerality / low stakes** — margin notes are throwaway; no pressure to
   produce an artifact.
4. **Spatial layout** — arrows, boxes, position-as-meaning; 2D rather than a
   linear stream.

The question: for an LLM-based agent (which has no motor system), does anything
play the same role **in symbolic meaning** — a practice that changes the
*process* of thinking, not just the storage of notes?

## Three asks

1. **Your independent mapping** — for each of the four mechanisms: does a
   meaningful agent analogue exist, what is it, or why does the mechanism not
   transfer? Disagree freely with the framing if it is wrong.
2. **Prior art you know** — research or engineering practice relevant to:
   sketch/diagram-first generation, forced-brevity intermediate drafts,
   ephemeral scratchpads, spatial/2D intermediate representations for LLMs.
   Name + approximate date + your confidence.
3. **One experiment** — design one concrete A/B experiment that would show
   whether one of these mechanisms causally improves an agent's output quality.
   Keep it runnable by a single person with one agent and blind human-or-model
   scoring.

## Output format

A single markdown file, ≤800 words, sections matching the three asks.
Mark speculation as speculation.
