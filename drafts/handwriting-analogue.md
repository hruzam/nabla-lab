## The impulse (majkee, in-chat)

Humans use small temporary hand-writings and personal journals. The fine motorics of writing are
driven from centres near speech production ("same centre as the tongue is driven") — handwriting
activates different parts than typing to a keyboard; the *process* itself helps thinking, and this
is underestimated. The lab already has this "in fact" (files, journals) — but is there something
that helps an agent the same way **in symbolic meaning**? Worth research: internet pass, or build
a testing area.

## First mapping (Oraculum, same session — hypotheses, NOT findings)

What handwriting buys the human brain — candidate ingredients (all `[t]` training-era, verify):

1. **Forced compression** — the hand is slower than thought; you cannot transcribe verbatim, you
   must select and compress. This is the strongest documented effect (Mueller & Oppenheimer 2014,
   "The Pen Is Mightier Than the Keyboard" `[t]`).
2. **Motor–semantic coupling** — writing engages motor-perception loops beyond language areas
   (Longcamp et al. `[t]`; van der Meer & van der Weel, EEG connectivity, ~2024 `[t]`).
3. **Ephemerality / low stakes** — margin notes are throwaway; freedom from artifact pressure.
4. **Spatial layout** — arrows, boxes, position-as-meaning; 2D, not a 1D token stream.

Agent analogues, per ingredient:

1. **Compression rituals** — token-capped scratch ("say it in 20 tokens first"); the one-line
   governing insight required before any artifact. The constraint plays the role of hand-speed.
2. **Modality shift** — re-encoding an idea as ASCII diagram / table / pseudo-formula pulls from
   different token distributions than prose: the closest thing to "another centre." Note: the
   pulse move-algebra (`.` `?` `!` `>`) is *already* a hand-note system in this exact sense.
3. **Ephemeral scratch** — session-scratch explicitly non-canonical (thinking space, never flag).
4. **A canvas** — this environment has a tldraw canvas MCP: a testing area is buildable *today*.
   Experiment: diagram-first (sketch the idea as shapes/arrows, then write) vs prose-first, same
   idea, N instrument cards, blind Janus scoring. Does the sketch phase measurably change quality?
